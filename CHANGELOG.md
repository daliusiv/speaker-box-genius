## 1.2.0 (2026-11-24)

### Changed
- Driver picker **Suits → Open baffle (passive)**, driver pages and the open baffle calculator now judge a driver without EQ by its own bass level at 40 Hz, worked out from Fs and Qts (−2 dB or better), instead of Qts 0.6 or more. A Qts 0.7 driver with Fs in the 40s gives no more bass on a board than a low-Qts DSP woofer, so the list is shorter and more honest. The calculator's worked example now uses the SB Acoustics SB34NRX75-16.
- Open baffle flat boards: the board's thickness now counts. An 18 mm board's rear wave goes round its side face, so it gives a little more bass and a slightly lower dipole peak than a thin one (on a 45 cm board about 0.8 dB in the bass, the peak about 8 % lower), checked against boundary-element solutions of nine thick boards within 0.13 dB in the bass and 2.4 % at the peak.
- Open baffle H- and U-frames: the frame's duct now opens like the end of a pipe rather than a hole in a wall, so its resonance and the bump it gives sit where boundary-element runs put them (about 8–10 % higher and up to 4 dB stronger than before). The quarter-wave readout moves with it.
- Open baffle H- and U-frames: the sound round the frame is now worked out from edge diffraction round the frame as a block (as a flat board's), checked against boundary-element solutions of four frames within about 0.3 dB in the bass and 0.5–0.8 dB rms. Frames were 1.6–2.5 dB too loud in the bass with their peak about 5 dB short. Path D, Dipole peak and First null on a frame's card come from the new model; a frame takes a second or so to work out, so its curves and numbers follow a moment after a change (in the app and the [open baffle calculator](https://speakerboxgenius.com/calculators/open-baffle/), without holding up the page).
- To find where people get stuck, **Help improve the app** now includes anonymous replays of how the app is used: no cookies, no ID that follows you, what you type into fields is hidden in your browser before sending, and replays are kept for 30 days. Switch it off any time in **Settings → Privacy** (it stops at once). See [Privacy](https://speakerboxgenius.com/privacy/).
- Dayton Audio and SB Acoustics drivers are checked against their websites every week, so new and updated models reach the database sooner.
- Guides: [matching an amplifier](https://speakerboxgenius.com/guides/amp-matching/) explains headroom (music's peaks against the ratings' averages), and [power compression](https://speakerboxgenius.com/guides/power-compression/) shows the heated coil, the coil temperature limit and the Power & heat chart.
- The charts' simulations run in the background: typing a value or dragging the drive slider no longer waits for them, and the charts update as soon as the new results are in. Adding, removing or switching a box on or off still updates at once.
- Easier reading on the guides, calculators, driver pages and the rest of the site: a thin line and more space before each section, roomier lists, tables in cards with lined-up numbers, roomier notes, quieter link underlines and a smaller Sources list. On a wide screen (1240 px and up) pages with three or more sections show **On this page** beside the text. A lone open button stays solid; in a row of buttons (equal choices, like the four wirings in the amp guide) each one is an outline.
- Charts use the room they have: on a computer each chart is drawn as wide as its column, so labels and lines stay their normal size instead of growing with the window, wide charts label more frequencies (15, 40, 70 … with faint lines between), and a chart's height follows the window (about 42 % of it, 260 to 480 px). Phones are as before.
- Tidier chart headers: one line with the chart's name, its buttons and ⓘ. What the curves are drawn at (drive, 1 m, half space, peak or RMS, first limit reached …) is a small note in the chart's top right corner, and "Hover or tap for values" is gone.
- The forum image draws its charts at the same size from any screen, with each chart's full title.
- Choose any chart for any place on a computer: each chart's name is a menu of every chart (grouped: response and limits, electrical, box, time), so the "More charts" tab row is gone. Picking a chart that's already showing swaps the two, and your choice is remembered. Phones keep their tabs.
- Wide screens use the width: the app now grows to 2400 px, and from about 1700 px it shows two charts a row (Response beside Cone excursion, Max SPL beside Impedance to start). **Charts per row** above the charts switches back to one a row.
- Two charts a row line up: the cards in a row are the same height, and a chart's note (low power, program signal, Za, impulse) now sits inside its card.
- The Measured card shows only once you've added a measurement. Add one with **Add a measured response (.frd)…** in the Response chart's menu or **Add a measured impedance (.zma)…** in the Impedance chart's menu (on a phone, **Open / Import**).
- Edit on the response chart is a pencil with "Edit", without a border, the same height as the Relative / dB SPL switch.
- Every guide has a picture: a line drawing on its card in the guides list and under its introduction. The MLTL and sealed Qtc guides now show their response chart when shared, like the others.
- Usage stats: opening the app from one of the site's own pages (a guide, a calculator, a driver page) now counts as "another page of this site" in where you arrived from. Before, those visits left it out. Still only that, never the address ([Privacy](https://speakerboxgenius.com/privacy/)).

The driver picker's **Suits** filter gains **Open baffle (passive)** (the driver's own level at 40 Hz, from Fs and Qts, −2 dB or better) and **Open baffle with DSP** (Qts ≥ 0.35, Xmax ≥ 8 mm, Fs ≤ 45 Hz).

A passive radiator you typed has **Submit to database…** in its card too. A maintainer checks each one against the datasheet before it goes in.

- Reset to the database: change a database driver's T/S values, Xmax, Pe, Le or coil model (or a database passive radiator's parameters) and a line under it says what was edited ("T/S was edited"), with **Reset** to put the published values and name back. The number of drivers, their wiring, your boxes and the volume calculator stay as they are, and Undo brings your values back. Works for the comparison driver too.
- Mmd: the T/S parameters have an Mmd box beside Mms, the moving mass without the air load (as Hornresp and some datasheets give it). Type either one and the other is calculated from Sd. Pasting a datasheet line like "Mmd 12.3 g" or "Moving mass without air load" now fills Mmd.
- Baffle step on a driver's curve: **Save & Export → Crossover tools → Driver on baffle**, opening an .frd (**Put the driver on my baffle**) or **Signal → Baffle step → Driver curve** opens a window that loads a driver's half-space .frd (from a datasheet or a ground-plane measurement), shows it on your baffle and the baffle's effect by itself, and **Export FRD** saves it with the baffle's diffraction (and phase) for VituixCAD, XSim or another crossover tool. **⋯ → Export baffle only** saves the baffle's effect alone. The baffle's size and the driver's place are Baffle step's own fields (editable in the window too), and it works with the baffle step off: the charts don't change. The cone diameter starts from the driver's Sd. The file stays in the window for the session and never goes into links or projects. See the [baffle step guide](https://speakerboxgenius.com/guides/baffle-step/).
- Passive radiator parameters show Fp, the PR's free-air resonance, to check against the datasheet's Fs.
- Driver database: a **Suits** filter (Sealed, Ported, Infinite baffle) from each driver's EBP and Qts, and two columns on wide screens, **EBP** (Fs/Qes) and **Vd** (Sd × Xmax, the air one driver can move, in cm³ or in³). Infinite baffle picks drivers for a wall or floor with an attic or the next room behind them: Fs up to 30 Hz with Qts 0.5 or more, or EBP under 50.
- Sealed boxes show Fc, Qtc (next to the driver's Qts, with how much the box raises it) and Vb ÷ Vas under the volume. From 10 × Vas a line says the box acts as an infinite baffle, and that the box leak setting counts as a gap into the room.
- Infinite baffle box type (**Boxes → + Box → Infinite baffle**): drivers in a wall, floor or ceiling with their backs to an **Open** attic, garage or outdoors (the system resonates at the driver's own Fs and Qts), or a **Closed space** of any volume, a room, cupboard or car boot (springs the cone like a very big sealed box; its leak goes elsewhere, never into the room). The card shows Fc, Qtc against Qts and, for a closed space, Rear ÷ Vas. An open rear has no Box pressure (nothing behind the cone), so it isn't drawn on that chart. Shared links, projects, Hornresp records (an open rear is a box record with no rear chamber, and opens back as one) and the Linkwitz Transform's Match sealed design work with it; WinISD has no infinite baffle, so its export leaves these designs out and says so. New guide: [Infinite baffle subwoofer](https://speakerboxgenius.com/guides/infinite-baffle/).
- Validation: the baffle step and edge diffraction are compared with The Edge 4.x on nine baffles (25 × 40 and 40 × 60 cm, centred and off-centre, point sources and 130 and 200 mm cones, at 1, 2 and 100 m): within 0.1 dB to 2 kHz for point sources and 0.3 dB for cones up to the frequency the app marks as approximate. See [Validation](https://speakerboxgenius.com/validation/#the-edge).
- Validation: VituixCAD agrees on the cone's and passive radiators' peak force (within 1.1 %), and its bandpass F3 is within 0.3 % of the app's once read off its curve (its labels are values on its frequency grid).
- Validation: Hornresp agrees on an open-rear infinite baffle (two drivers, within 0.03 dB); the SPICE circuit check covers infinite-baffle mounting (a driver in a wall, its back to an open attic or a closed rear space whose leak isn't heard), 444 cases in all.
- Validation: Hornresp's passive Filter Wizard agrees on Signal → Passive parts (a coil with its resistance in line and a capacitor across, on a ported box: within 0.03 dB, 0.3 % in impedance).
- Car wedge lip: **Build → Box → Outside size** has a **Lip height** for an upright strip under the slope (0 = sloped all the way down, as before). The volume, slope angle, side drawing (with its L size), 3D view, cut list (a Lip panel and the lip joint's angle), FreeCAD macro and driver-fit check all follow it, and **Size for a volume** can solve for the lip.
- Leakage per box: sealed, ported and passive radiator cards have a **Leakage** row. **Same as Settings** uses Settings' Leak Ql (10 by default, as WinISD); **This box** gives the box its own Ql, so a tight box and a leaky one can be compared side by side. The passive radiator finder, Best tuning, the stuffing fit and the WinISD and SPICE exports use the box's own Ql.
- Amplifier power limit: **Settings → Limits → Amplifier → Max power** (W) is a third amplifier limit beside Max volts and Max current, for an amp's continuous power (its power supply or heat). It counts the real power the amp sends into the speaker's impedance at each frequency, so it allows more voltage where the impedance is high and less at its dips; the lowest of the three applies. A line under it gives the watts in volts and amps into the nominal load. The Max SPL chart has a **Watts** view (the power the amp must deliver to reach max SPL, with your limit as a dashed line), and its tooltip names which amp limit sets the maximum (amp volts, amp current or amp power).
- Headroom on a program signal: the Max SPL chart's **Program** button (a bottom sheet on phones) turns Program on, with a **Signal** (Sine, Pink noise, Music, Cinema or Custom), its **Crest factor** (peak ÷ RMS; a sine is 3 dB) and an optional **Target SPL**. Each box's curve then shows the loudest it plays on that signal: limits that hold the peaks (Xmax, port speed, air, amp volts and current) come sooner by the crest factor, those that hold the average (the driver's power rating, amp watts) don't. Headroom from each box's F3 up is shown **On peaks** and **On average** in the legend, the hover box and the summary table (amber under 3 dB, red short of the target), with the target as a dashed line and the tightest point ringed. The build sheet prints it too. Off by default: Max SPL is the sine curve as before.

- Cone and passive radiator velocity, acceleration and force: a **mm · m/s · g · N** switch on the Cone excursion chart (a menu on phones) shows the excursion as velocity, acceleration (in g) or force (moving mass × acceleration, the push each cone or PR puts into the box, as VituixCAD's peak force); the PR excursion chart follows it. Red still marks excursion past Xmax, and Xmax is drawn as a dashed curve in the chosen unit; the readout keeps the excursion in mm and % of Xmax. The CSV export has the three new curves for every cone and PR group.

- Excursion units: the excursion charts' switch also shows **%** (each cone's or PR's excursion as a share of its own Xmax, so they read on one scale) and **cm³** (the air each one moves, Sd × excursion; in³ with imperial units), and excursion follows **Settings → Units** (inches with imperial). **Settings → Limits → Port speed & excursion → Excursion** shows excursion, and the velocity, acceleration and force views, as the peak one way (the default, as before), peak-to-peak or RMS: the charts, the Xmax line and its label, the summary table, the forum table and the CSV follow it. It only changes what's shown: every limit is still checked on the one-way peak.
- Anonymous crash reports: when the app hits an error, it sends the error and where it happened to PostHog's EU servers, so it can be fixed before anyone has to report it. No cookies, no page views or clicks, never your design (the part after the `#`), and nothing if your browser sends Do Not Track. See [Privacy](https://speakerboxgenius.com/privacy/).
